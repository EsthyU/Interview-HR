To the Participant

Thank you for agreeing to take part in this research. This form contains interview questions for a study on how engineers' online professional identities particularly on LinkedIn and similar platforms are evaluated during hiring processes.

Please read each question carefully and write your responses in the fields provided. You do not need to answer all questions if some do not apply to your experience simply write "not applicable" or leave the field blank.

There are no right or wrong answers. I am interested in your professional reasoning and lived experience, not textbook answers.

  • 1
    Complete in one sitting or across several sessions no time limit
  • 2
    Write as much or as little as you feel comfortable with
  • 3
    If a question prompts a follow-up thought, add it at the end
  • 4
    Your name and any identifying details will be anonymised before analysis

Consent

  • You have read and understood the purpose of this study.
  • Your participation is voluntary and you may withdraw at any time without consequence.
  • Your responses will be used only for research purposes and will be anonymised in all reporting.
  • You are happy for your written responses to be quoted (without identifying you) in research outputs.
1
Your Background
These questions help me understand your professional context. They are not evaluative.
1
Please describe your current role and your organisation. What types of engineering positions do you hire for, and at what seniority levels?
Which markets or regions do you recruit in?
2
Roughly how many engineering hires have you been involved in over the past two to three years?
Were these mainly junior, mid-level, or senior roles?
2
Your Hiring Workflow
How you approach the hiring process from start to shortlist.
3
Walk me through your typical hiring workflow from receiving an application to creating a shortlist. At what point, if at all, do you check a candidate's online presence?
Is this a routine step or something you do only in certain situations?
4
Which online platforms do you check for engineering candidates for example LinkedIn, GitHub, personal portfolios, ResearchGate, or others and why those particular ones?
Are there platforms you have tried and found unhelpful or stopped using?
3
Evaluating Online Profiles
How you read and interpret what you find online.
5
When you open a candidate's profile for the first time, what are the first three to five things you look at to judge their credibility and fit? Why do those things matter to you?
Can you walk through a recent example what did you look at, and in what order?
6
What separates a strong online professional profile from a weak one for engineers? Please describe examples of each from your experience.
What made the strong profile stand out? Was the weak profile a dealbreaker, or just a neutral factor?
7
How do you actually use information from a candidate's online presence during screening and evaluation? In what situations does it help you, and when does it raise concerns?
Has a candidate's online presence ever changed a decision you were already leaning toward? What happened?
4
Credibility, Trust & Status Signals
How you judge whether what you see online is genuine, and what signals carry weight.
8
Do status or prestige signals show up in your evaluation? For example: well-known employers, prestigious universities, certifications, awards, or international experience. Which feel like meaningful indicators, and which feel like noise or even a red flag?
Is there a signal that looks impressive but that you have learned not to trust?
9
What makes you trust that a candidate's profile is accurate and authentic? What patterns make you suspect exaggeration or inflated claims?
What is the exact detail that triggers doubt for you?
10
Do you pay attention to platform-level signals for example follower count, post engagement, or posting frequency? If so, how do you interpret them?
Is a high follower count a positive signal, a neutral one, or something that gives you pause?
5
Personal Branding & Visibility Behaviours
How you interpret candidates' deliberate self-presentation online.
11
What kinds of visibility or personal branding behaviours do you notice among candidates for example frequent posting, collecting certifications, or carefully curated achievement highlights? How do you interpret those behaviours when you see them?
Does it change your view if the activity seems very recent or appears to have started just before a job search? Is there a version of this behaviour you find credible versus one that feels performative?
12
If you have hired across different markets or have knowledge of how hiring differs between contexts like Nigeria and Romania, or local versus international markets have you noticed differences in what is considered credible online?
Are there signals that read well in one context but raise questions in another? Skip this question if it does not apply to your experience.
6
Practical Advice
Reflecting and synthesising your experience.
13
If you could give engineers three concrete, practical rules for building an online presence that genuinely supports their hiring outcomes, what would they be?
Is there anything you wish more candidates understood about how their profiles are actually read?
